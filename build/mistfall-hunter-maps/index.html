How many maps are in Mistfall Hunter?

For a quick answer, say two named areas: Hallowgrove and Brandrgarde. That is the clearest way to describe the broad locations players discuss. A player asking which map to enter is usually asking about one of those names, not about every difficulty or queue variation attached to it.

For a precise route, use a more detailed count. Community map databases can show Hallowgrove, Brandrgarde North, and Brandrgarde South as three separate map views. Those labels are useful when you need the correct sheet for a run. They do not prove that the game publisher classifies North and South as two additional named regions. Treat “two areas” and “three map views” as answers to two different questions, then check the exact view shown in your current lobby.

The difference matters because a map name, an area layout, a difficulty, and a queue are not interchangeable. If someone says there are three maps, ask whether they mean the three community atlas sheets. If they say there are two, they are likely counting Hallowgrove and Brandrgarde at the named-area level. Neither count should be stretched to include every mode as another map.

Why some Mistfall Hunter maps guides say two and others say three

The difference comes from page structure. Broad guides group Hallowgrove and Brandrgarde as the two named hunting areas. Map databases often need a separate image or data layer for each playable layout, so they present Hallowgrove plus Brandrgarde North and Brandrgarde South. That view is handy for markers and route notes, but it is a community database’s organization rather than a developer-published map-count statement.

When you compare sources, look at what the page calls a map. A hub may count region names. A community atlas may count map sheets. A game menu may list difficulty and Solo or Trio options under an area. These are related labels, but they answer different questions. This guide uses “area” for the two broad names and “view” for a North or South atlas sheet to prevent the counts from drifting together.

Do not infer that every version of the game exposes three independent top-level entries just because a community site has three tabs. Conversely, do not assume the North and South data can be overlaid on one page if the run uses a specific view. Match your route notes to the exact current area label before leaving camp.

Hallowgrove vs. Brandrgarde: what the two area names tell you

Hallowgrove is described in launch coverage as the opening area, with forest paths, ruins, hiding places, and treasure to discover. That makes it a natural place to learn the basic rhythm of entering, reading a fight, and deciding when to leave. This is practical guidance based on the area’s role in the game and reported play, not a guarantee that every Hallowgrove queue is easy or safe.

Brandrgarde is the other broad area name and is represented by separate North and South views in community atlases. It has also received a meaningful queue change since launch: Bellring Games added Solo Mode to Brandrgarde’s Normal and Cataclysm difficulties in the September 3, 2026 live update. If a guide still says Brandrgarde is only available in Trio, check its publication date before relying on that advice.

The broad choice is therefore not simply “beginner map versus team map.” Your current mode, entry rules, gear, objective, and comfort with the route matter too. The safest useful preparation is to confirm the area and queue in the client, then use a map sheet only if its label matches the run you are about to start.

Map modes changed: Brandrgarde is no longer trio-only

A common outdated note says that Brandrgarde requires a trio. Bellring Games’ September 3, 2026 live update says Solo Mode was added for Brandrgarde’s Normal and Cataclysm difficulties. The change affects how players can queue for those modes; it does not create a new geographic region or turn Solo into an offline mode.

This is why map advice needs a date. A guide written before the September update may correctly describe the earlier queue rules and still be wrong for a current run. When a recommendation depends on team size, confirm the queue in the game client and look for a source newer than the patch. The official announcement is the best source for the date and the modes it names.

Difficulty labels also do not increase the number of broad map names. A player can discuss a Brandrgarde difficulty or solo queue without meaning a different map. Keep the map, difficulty, and team-size choice as separate checks in your pre-run plan.

A simple Mistfall Hunter map plan for your next run

A map guide is most useful when it changes one decision before you queue. You do not need to memorize every marker. Decide what the run is for, where you will stop taking optional risks, and what you will do if the first route becomes unsafe. The steps below are a player-facing planning method, not a hidden in-game rule or a guaranteed extraction route.

For a first visit, keep the plan short. Pick one objective and one fallback. If you are testing a new build, keep the gear plan conservative enough that one bad encounter does not erase the whole experiment. The site’s loadout planner can help you set a role and risk direction before you enter.

  1. Confirm Hallowgrove or Brandrgarde, then confirm the current difficulty and Solo or Trio queue in the game client.
  2. Choose one objective that fits the time and gear you brought; do not turn a single objective into a full-map sweep by default.
  3. Before moving toward a fight or marker, identify a fallback direction and an extraction option that still makes sense if the encounter changes.
  4. Use a North or South community map view only when it matches the Brandrgarde layout shown for your run.
  5. After extracting or losing the run, note one route decision to keep and one to change; update only what the result supports.

Are there three Mistfall Hunter maps?

Can you play Brandrgarde solo?

Does a difficulty count as another map?

How many players or teams are in each map?
